A true-to-scale watch try-on

Try any watch on your wrist at true size, with the error bar to prove it.

Case diameter alone doesn't tell you how a watch will wear. Lug to Lug measures your wrist from a single photo, using the watch you already wear as the ruler, then shows any other watch on it at its real size, with the sizing error printed on the render.

The idea

The watch you're wearing is a ruler.

Other virtual try-ons guess the scale from the image or ask you to place the watch by hand. Lug to Lug measures it instead, and says how sure it is.

01 · Recognise

Find the watch you have on

It reads the dial, searches catalogue photos and matches reference numbers, then asks you to confirm. Its case diameter becomes the known length in the photo.

02 · Calibrate

Turn the ellipse into millimetres

An ellipse fitted to the case outline gives pixels per millimetre and the wrist's tilt. The outline's uncertainty becomes a ± mm on every number that follows.

03 · Render

Show the next watch at its real size

The new watch is drawn to its published dimensions and posed on your wrist. The old one is painted out, the light is matched, and a fit report checks lug overhang and thickness.

The proof

Every claim gets a benchmark.

Swap tests produce ground truth from ordinary photos. A set of reference watches is measured with calipers, and several volunteers wear them in a fixed pose.

Step 1

Photograph watch A

On a wrist, from an overhead mount, so the pose stays fixed.

Step 2

Render watch B from that photo

Calibrated from A alone, exactly as the app would do it for a visitor.

Step 3

Compare with the real photo of B

Diameter error in millimetres and outline overlap, on watches and wrists never used for tuning.

What it will do

Three ways to see it, and help deciding.

Every render path keeps the size and the dial exactly as measured. Everything around them is there to help you choose.

CPU · default

Photo try-on

A composite on your own photo in seconds, with its ± mm and a fit report.

GPU · optional

Studio

Photoreal relighting with the dial and case face locked, so the size can't drift.

On your phone

Live AR

Hand tracking in the browser, scaled from your measured wrist, rendered on the device.

Fit report and search by size

Lug overhang, thickness and how large it wears next to watches you own. Ask in plain English: "a 38–40 mm GMT under 12 mm thick".

Swipe deck, assistant and MCP server

A deck that learns your size and style, an assistant that answers sizing questions with sources, and the same tools for other AI applications.

Scales to zero

CPU first, GPU optional and capped. Every service sleeps when nobody is using it, behind a monthly budget alert.

Keyless, reproducible delivery

Every cloud resource is in Terraform. GitHub Actions deploys through workload identity federation, so no service-account key exists.

Private by design

Written consent before any photo, pseudonymous IDs, raw photos kept out of the repository, uploads processed in memory with location data stripped.

Measured, not asserted

Frozen test splits grouped by watch and by person, 95% intervals on every rate, and results reported by slice, thin slices included.
